Mobile apps

Native iOS and Android apps for media and sport, built for engagement, loyalty and daily habit rather than just another channel.

VerbaVista powers publisher apps with digital editions, paywalls and subscription management. FanFuel powers club, league and federation apps with memberships, loyalty programmes and gamification.

How our partners work

KRIK

KRIK breaks investigative stories that other outlets republish within minutes, taking the traffic with them. Their app puts each story on readers’ phones the moment it publishes, so the audience arrives at KRIK first rather than at whoever copied it fastest. Direct push replaced a dependency on platforms and aggregators.

Read the story

DAN

DAN was one of Montenegro’s strongest newspapers in print, with an audience that had moved to phones. Digital editions, subscriptions and a mobile app carried the transition without giving up the editorial identity that made the paper matter in the first place. Print heritage turned into a digital position rather than a liability.

Read the story

ESAKE

Leagues face the club problem multiplied: fixtures, results and standings across dozens of teams, and fans who want all of it live. The Greek Basket League app runs largely on autopilot — articles come from the league’s own website, live scores and statistics sync straight from the data provider, and gamification keeps fans engaged between matchdays. Nobody on staff updates anything by hand.

Read the story

HKS

The Croatian Basketball Association covers men’s and women’s leagues, junior competitions and the national teams, and each fan follows only part of that. Games, standings, box scores and play-by-play arrive from the data provider, news and video from the federation’s own channels, and notifications are switchable per competition — so following one league doesn’t mean hearing about all of them. Live match alerts update in place instead of firing again for every basket.

Read the story

SAT

SAT built a following of car and motoring enthusiasts who wanted more than articles to read. The app turns reading into participation — points for engagement, rewards for loyalty, and content shaped around what each follower actually cares about. It gives a committed niche audience a reason to keep coming back rather than passing through.

Read the story

Boom 93

Boom 93 is an institution in Požarevac, and its audience is local by definition. The app opens with content from the reader’s own city, and quizzes built around rock music let a legendary radio station play to the identity it already has. Local relevance became a feature rather than a constraint.

Read the story

Hanza Media

Hanza Media publishes a portfolio of newspapers and magazines, each with its own subscriber base. The e-kiosk brings all of it into one place — digital subscriptions, an e-paper reader, and an app where a subscriber finds every title they pay for. One system instead of one per publication.

Read the story

Politika

Politika is the oldest daily newspaper in Serbia, and both its daily edition and its subscription business needed to work on a phone. The webshop and e-kiosk handle subscriptions and delivery, and the app puts every issue in a reader’s pocket. A title with more than a century of history, distributed the way people read now.

Read the story

Večer

Večer wanted an app built for readers who pay, not for casual traffic. Premium content, the daily e-paper and a web kiosk sit behind a single subscription, so the app serves subscribers instead of chasing clicks. A publisher that decided depth was worth more than reach.

Explore Litteraworks
How our partners work

Insajder

Insajder’s investigative programmes were yielding one or two social clips per episode, because cutting them by hand took time nobody had. Automatic clip generation with subtitles now produces two to three times that from the same episode, for Instagram, TikTok and YouTube Shorts. The reporting reaches audiences that would never sit through the full programme.

National broadcasters

Three national public broadcasters run Litteraworks inside their daily editorial workflow. Every broadcast hour is speech that has to become text — transcripts, subtitles, an archive somebody can actually search. Work that used to be typed is now reviewed.

Ringier Serbia

Ringier Serbia’s journalists were watching live television to find the quotes worth writing about. Litteraworks transcribes the broadcast as it airs, so an editor searches text instead of scrubbing video and drafts the article straight from the transcript. Hours of watching became minutes of reading.

News agencies

For a news agency, minutes decide whether a story is theirs. These agencies use Litteraworks to turn recorded audio into publishable text fast enough to matter — press conferences, statements and interviews transcribed while the event is still live.

Sports clubs

Press conferences, interviews and manager reactions arrive as audio and have to become content the same day. Both clubs use Litteraworks to transcribe, translate, draft articles and cut subtitled clips, so the media team spends its time editing rather than typing. Matchday content that keeps up with matchday.

Star

Star wanted every article on the site available as audio, in Greek. Litteraworks text-to-speech provides natural-sounding Greek voices across the whole publication, so readers can listen instead of read. One text, two ways to consume it, and a real accessibility gain.

Media Asset Management

Pchela

AI-powered media asset management that makes your entire archive searchable and accessible in seconds.

Find anything instantly. From video footage to documents - AI-powered search that actually understands what you're looking for.

Explore Pchela
How our partners work

Superliga Srbije

Superliga Srbije produces photo and video from every matchday, media day and league event, and all of it has to reach the clubs. That used to mean WhatsApp groups and WeTransfer links that expired; now every club works from one searchable archive with permanent access. The league’s archive and its daily distribution became the same system.

FK Partizan

FK Partizan’s photographers shoot from the pitch and upload straight into Pchela, where an image is ready to publish and share with media within minutes. The same archive holds the club’s historical photo and video collection, searchable by what’s actually in the file rather than by folder name. Daily workflow and long-term archive in one place.

Media 24

Media 24’s photographers shoot across the country, and every image has to reach the newsroom while it still matters. They upload from the field directly into Pchela, where AI tagging makes each photo findable the moment it lands, and journalists pull what they need without asking anyone. Nothing sits between the shoot and the story.
